## Confirmed direction

- Build an RPG universe with The Legend of Zelda and World of Warcraft as broad reference points.
- World concept: a living frontier built on another world.
- World approach: colourful, mysterious adventure.
- Selected story direction: The Returning Kingdom. A buried civilisation restores its old landscape, threatening the new world, while the original catastrophe remains capable of destroying the world itself.
- The protagonist stumbles upon a world-ending threat as the game progresses.
- The story should be epic, with an increasingly alarming journey and multiple story hooks at each stage.
- Three endings depend on player actions: good, bad, and neutral.
- Bad ending: the near-default main-story path. The player joins the evil, becomes the World Destroyer, and the original catastrophe destroys the world.
- Neutral ending: the player refuses the destruction; immediate annihilation fails, but the old kingdom erases the new world. The original catastrophe remains possible.
- Good ending: old and new worlds merge and both are preserved. The world-destroying threat is permanently destroyed.
- Reaching either neutral or good requires serious effort from the player.
- Inhabited maps combine open-world exploration, three settlements per map, and a few dungeons each.

## Tentative world scope

- Three inhabited regional maps, for nine settlements in total if this scope is retained.
- Two connecting maps with a larger geographical scale and no towns.
- Exact dimensions, dungeon counts, and travel times remain undecided.

## Creative references

For the cause of the catastrophe and the world's deeper history, explore the combination of Robin Hobb's Farseer trilogy and Brandon Sanderson's Mistborn and Stormlight Archive series. The aim is an original setting that remains recognisable to fantasy RPG players.

## Open decisions

- The original catastrophe, the buried kingdom's detailed history, and how surface societies developed.
- The restoration spell's exact rules, limits, power source, and escalation.
- Magic rules, costs, and their relationship to the catastrophe.
- Protagonist, personal stakes, cultures, factions, and antagonist.
- Exact requirements, costs, branching conditions, and final scenes for the three confirmed endings.
- Region and settlement names, identities, and histories.
- Player mode, combat model, production scope, and technical implementation.
