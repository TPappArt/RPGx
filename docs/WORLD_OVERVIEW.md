# RPGx World Overview

The Returning Kingdom

Version 0.3 | 13 October 2026 | Project owner Tibor Papp

This living document defines RPGx and gives game builders a shared reference. A colourful, mysterious frontier stands above sleeping cities belonging to the people now called the Ancients. Long before them, three world-sustaining races sacrificed themselves to imprison the Formless. The Ancients later triggered a second catastrophe and entered suspended animation. Their restoration threatens today's world, while the Formless remain capable of destroying everything. Player actions determine destruction, replacement, or lasting coexistence.

## World foundation

### Confirmed premise and three historical layers

The Returning Kingdom remains the selected story direction. Its inhabitants are called **the Ancients** by the people of the present world. This is a modern umbrella name, not a confirmed name used by the Ancients themselves.

1. **The age of the protectors:** Three extremely long-lived but not immortal races dwell deep within the earth, high in the sky, and beneath the water. They sustain and protect the world as a balancing triumvirate. The Formless threatens all existence. The protectors imprison it in a separate plane at the cost of their own lives.
2. **The age of the Ancients:** Thousands of years after the protectors' final stand, one or more highly advanced peoples achieve extraordinary mastery of their world and magic. They encounter a trap left by the Formless. Their interaction with it triggers a cataclysm. Their cities survive behind powerful protective barriers only because their people enter near-lifeless magical slumber. The enclosed cities sink or become buried over approximately 10,000 years.
3. **The present age:** New civilizations and competing kingdoms arise. Their technology, politics, and everyday culture are broadly medieval-fantasy in character. They inhabit a colourful, mysterious living frontier above the Ancients' lost domain.

The present-day races know **nothing** of the Worldbound or the Formless. They have a term for the lost civilization, "the Ancients", but do not possess the true chronology or explanation of its disappearance. Discovering those truths belongs to the story, not to ordinary surface-world knowledge.

### The three world-sustaining races

The collective working name **Worldbound** and the term **Threefold** for their alliance are provisional. The existence of three primordial races and their roles is confirmed; their actual names, individual appearances, cultures, and specific abilities are still in development.

- **Deep earth:** Ancient powers dwelling far below the surface, associated with the world's foundations and stability.
- **Sky:** Ancient powers dwelling high above the world, balancing the forces governing its upper reaches.
- **Underwater:** Ancient powers dwelling in the deep waters, balancing the forces of the seas and the life they sustain.

They were almost unimaginably powerful, not truly immortal. Their defining relationship was mutual correction: when one group pushed the world out of balance, the others pulled it back. They were the world's protectors, not merely three rival kingdoms.

All three are driven to extinction through their struggle against the Formless. They collectively succeed in imprisoning it in another plane, but the prison is not invulnerable. Remnants of their existence survive, including objects, memories, and special places. Some remnants grant limited access to impressions or power associated with the protectors; other contact points may allow the Formless to reach or influence the physical world. The exact rules and costs of contact are open.

### The Formless and the first catastrophe

**The Formless** is the established name of the destructive evil. It is associated with hatred and destructive emotions and seeks the destruction of everything. Whether these emotions literally create the Formless, nourish it, or give it a means to act is not yet mechanically defined.

The protectors trap the Formless in a different plane of existence. This victory prevents immediate annihilation but does not eliminate the threat. The prison has weaknesses and can be interfered with. The Formless remains dangerous even when unable to manifest freely.

The nature of the Formless as one intelligence, many entities, or a changing collective remains undecided. Its method of influencing minds, matter, and magical systems is also open. These unknowns should support gradual revelation.

### The Ancients and the second catastrophe

**The Ancients** is the present world's name for the lost, highly developed civilization and its people. Their original collective name and the names or number of component races remain open. Its original historical name remains undecided.

Thousands of years after the protectors' sacrifice, the Ancients reach an extraordinary level of development, including powerful magic and deep understanding of their world. They find an artifact, portal, or other ancient construct placed or manipulated as a trap by the Formless. They interfere with it, believing it offers knowledge or power, and trigger a cataclysm capable of destroying the world. The exact object and mechanism remain open.

The Ancients create protective barriers around their cities. These shields hold, but maintaining the defense while their inhabitants live normally would exhaust the systems that keep the catastrophe at bay. They enter a deep, near-lifeless magical sleep to minimize the drain. Their cities remain protected but are engulfed by the ground over roughly 10,000 years.

The preservation and restoration system is capable of bringing back the Ancients' cities and lost geography. Its designers did not provide a safe way to accommodate the societies now living on the surface. Restoring an ancient river, forest, or city can destroy the modern settlement occupying the same space. The Ancients are still living people with families, histories, interests, and claims, not inherently evil monsters.

Whether the modern races descended in any way from the Ancients is unconfirmed. The current races' existence is not dependent on that explanation.

### Why the crisis begins now

Frontier excavators open a buried sanctuary and awaken a caretaker of the Ancients. Following its ancient duty, it begins restoration. Each awakened city helps activate others; the network extends beyond the playable maps. What the caretaker knows about the original trap and about the world above remains open.

The Formless remains imprisoned but dangerous. The precise relationship between the returning cities, the strain on the prison, the surviving protector remnants, and a renewed world-destroying event still needs development.

### What is at stake

Two distinct threats determine the endings:

- **Restoration:** The Ancients and their geography return, potentially erasing the people and places of the present world.
- **Destruction:** The Formless can bring about the catastrophe that destroys the world itself.

Stopping immediate world destruction does not automatically prevent the Ancients from replacing the present civilization. Conversely, the return of the Ancients does not destroy the Formless. The good ending must resolve both threats.

The conflict can support negotiation, divided loyalties, and opposition within both the Ancients and the present-day kingdoms. Neither group's desire to survive automatically makes it morally right to erase the other.

### Tone and creative references

The established approach is a colourful, mysterious adventure with an epic story and an increasingly alarming journey. Zelda and World of Warcraft are game references; Farseer, Mistborn, and Stormlight are literary references. Tibor's Arthas comparison guides the protagonist's possible fall into the World Destroyer role. The Formless is now identified as the overarching evil; its exact nature and its relationship to the caretaker remain open. These references do not establish borrowed lore or specific gameplay systems.

## World structure and discovery

### Map scope

Each inhabited regional map combines open-world exploration, three settlements, and a few dungeons. The current total is tentative. Names, cultures, travel times, dimensions, and exact dungeon counts are open.

| Map type | Current scope | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inhabited regions | Three regions, three settlements each; nine settlements if retained | Tentative total |
| Connecting maps | Two larger maps without towns | Tentative total |
| Dungeons | A few in each inhabited region | Count open |

### Working sequence of discovery

The following sequence organises the approved premise for development. Its exact events and placement across maps are working proposals.

- Wonder: the player encounters a lively frontier and ordinary local needs. Early signs of restoration can appear useful or beautiful.

- Unease: separate incidents suggest something is wrong. Evidence begins contradicting local explanations and recorded history.

- Recognition: the player learns that the changes belong to the Ancients' older landscape and that its inhabitants are waking. The existence of the Worldbound and the Formless remains a later, harder revelation.

- Escalation: restoration threatens familiar communities, while evidence of the imprisoned Formless emerges as a distinct world-destroying danger. Conflicting interests complicate responses to both.

- Consequence: accumulated actions determine the protagonist's allegiance and whether the world is destroyed, the old kingdom replaces the new, or both survive together.

### Possible signs and story hooks

These examples illustrate the selected concept. They are candidates for later quest design rather than fixed events.

- A dry well refills and unfamiliar flowers spread through farmland. The apparent blessing marks the return of an ancient watercourse.

- An old road emerges beneath the grass and leads towards a buried entrance. Its route crosses places the current map treats as impassable.

- A restored river threatens a village that did not exist when the spell was created.

- A family pendant is recognised by someone in a newly opened ruin, creating a personal connection across the centuries.

Each developed hook should specify the immediate local problem, evidence the player can find, decisions available, and later consequences. The precise operation of these events must follow the magic rules once agreed.


## Race and civilization design register

This section distinguishes confirmed racial history from working design proposals. The present-day playable roster is **not decided**.

### Confirmed ancient racial structure

- **Primordial triumvirate:** Exactly three world-sustaining protector races, associated with deep earth, sky, and underwater realms. Their existence, balancing purpose, near-immortality, extinction, and sacrifice are canon.
- **The Ancients:** An advanced lost civilization, possibly comprising multiple races. "Ancients" is what present-day cultures call them, not their authentic self-name. Their numbers, appearances, cultures, and internal divisions are undecided.
- **Present-day races:** New civilizations in medieval-stage rival kingdoms. They have no knowledge of the Worldbound or the Formless. The number and identities of playable and non-playable races are undecided.

### Working protector-race concepts (not canon)

| Realm | Proposed race name | Proposed identity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep earth | Dhoruun | Stone or mineral-like beings linked to stability, structure, and the world's foundations |
| Sky | Aevori | Ethereal high-altitude beings linked to atmosphere, motion, and transformation |
| Underwater | Namar | Deep-water beings linked to life cycles, adaptation, and memory |

Worldbound and Threefold are also working names. Do not assume these biological traits, names, or powers are approved until confirmed.

### Working present-day race candidates (not canon)

Humans; Brann (mountain builders); Veyli (forest canopy dwellers); Nymari (amphibious coastal people); and Keth (nomadic peoples) are brainstormed visual and cultural directions only. No proposed roster size, appearance, ancestry, gameplay bonuses, or political alignment has been selected. Future development should prioritize distinct silhouettes, believable cultures, and meaningful relationships to the modern world rather than forced bloodlines to the protectors.

## Endings and future development

### Confirmed ending structure

Three endings depend on player actions. Bad is the almost unavoidable main-story trajectory unless the player seriously works towards another outcome. Neutral and good both require sustained effort. The exact branching conditions, thresholds, and point of commitment remain undecided.

### Confirmed ending outcomes

These outcomes replace the earlier provisional endings. Their underlying events are canon; the quests, costs, mechanisms, and final scenes still need development.

| Ending | Confirmed outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Bad | The player joins the evil and becomes the World Destroyer. The original catastrophe comes to pass and the world is destroyed. This is the most prevalent path. |
| Neutral | The player refuses to join the destruction. World destruction fails for now, but the old kingdom destroys and replaces the new world. The ancient order returns; the catastrophe remains a future possibility. |
| Good | Old and new worlds merge, preserving both. The world-destroying threat is completely and permanently destroyed. Neither civilisation must be erased for the other to survive. |

Proposed design principle: give players understandable evidence and meaningful opportunities to change course. The harder endings should emerge from sustained decisions and preparation. Their precise mechanisms still need agreement.


### Questions to resolve next

- What are the real names, physical designs, histories, and abilities of the three protector races? Do the Worldbound and Threefold working labels survive?
- What is the true nature of the Formless? Does hatred create it, feed it, or allow it to manifest? What are its limits and weaknesses?
- What exactly was the trap the Ancients encountered, and how did it trigger the second catastrophe?
- What did the Ancients call themselves, how many races comprised their civilization, and what did they believe had happened to the protectors?
- How does the restoration network work, and what brings the Formless closer to escape?
- Who is the caretaker, what does it know, and how do returning rulers relate to the surface inhabitants?
- Which present-day races, kingdoms, and cultures are playable or non-playable?
- What draws the protagonist to the World Destroyer role? Which sustained actions enable refusing it or preserving both worlds?
- What makes the good ending's permanent destruction of the Formless possible?
